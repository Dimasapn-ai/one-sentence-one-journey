# One Sentence, One Journey — entry notes

## Final sentence

> Old Tomas rowed out before sunrise to scatter his wife's ashes beyond the reef, fought the squall that tore his sail and nearly took the urn with it, and came back at noon with the empty jar on the seat beside him and her laugh, unmistakably, riding the wind off the water.

## Candidates drafted

1. She left Lisbon at dawn with her father's compass and a ticket bought in anger, crossed three winters of other people's couches and one sea that took her brother's name off her tongue, and came home on the night train smelling of rain and diesel, able at last to say the name aloud.
2. He walked out of the mill town at seventeen with a harmonica and his mother's curse ringing in his ears, rode freight through forty summers of dust and diner coffee, and stepped off the last train at sixty with the same harmonica, playing the tune she used to hum while she forgave him.
3. The letter left Aleppo in a coat lining, crossed the sea in a fisherman's boot and two borders in a Bible's hollow spine, and reached her grandson's hands in Toronto forty years later, still smelling of cedar and smoke, its first line reading: begin again.
4. Mara sold the house the morning the ferry foghorn swallowed her husband's goodbye, followed the gulls north through a decade of rented rooms and burnt toast in strangers' kitchens, and bought it back at dusk with her daughter beside her, the key still warm from the estate agent's palm.
5. He set out to find the source of the river with a tin cup and his grandfather's map, drank from a hundred wells and one poisoned spring that cost him a year of memory, and found at the mountain's root not water but the village well he had left, running clear.
6. The violin left Cremona in 1712 in a merchant's cart, slept through wars in attics and a pawnshop's velvet dark, and sang again in a subway station in Queens, where the busker who had carried it across an ocean wept at the first note because it sounded like his mother.
7. She boarded the wrong train in Jaipur with forty rupees and a stranger's wedding invitation, rode it through monsoon country and a landslide that buried the tracks behind her, and arrived three days late at a wedding that turned out, by every account including the bride's, to be her own.
8. **(winner)** Old Tomas rowed out before sunrise to scatter his wife's ashes beyond the reef, fought the squall that tore his sail and nearly took the urn with it, and came back at noon with the empty jar on the seat beside him and her laugh, unmistakably, riding the wind off the water.
9. The seed left the burned valley in a child's pocket, crossed the drought in a tin of buttons and a winter in a widow's Bible, and broke ground the spring the rains returned, in soil its planter's grandchildren would call by her name.
10. He carried the last bottle of his father's wine across three countries and one war to pour it on a grave in Odessa, and when the cork finally sighed open, he drank the first glass himself, standing in the rain, forgiving them both.

## Why the winner wins

- **Whole arc, not a list:** one continuous motion in a single breath — row out (departure), fight the squall (the middle, with something genuinely at stake: the urn itself), come back at noon (arrival). Sunrise → noon brackets the journey in time as well as space.
- **A real turn:** he sets out to let her go and returns carrying her anyway — not the ashes, but her laugh. The person is changed, not just relocated; grief becomes company.
- **Concrete detail:** named man (Old Tomas), named things (reef, sail, urn, the empty jar on the seat beside him), and a sound to finish — her laugh riding the wind off the water.
- **Final clause lands:** the sentence saves its weight for the end. "Unmistakably" does the emotional work in one word, and the last image is sound, not summary — the judge feels the arrival instead of being told about it.
- **Discipline:** exactly one sentence, comfortably inside the 300-character gate; no length for its own sake — every clause either moves the boat or turns the man.

Runners-up 3 and 5 were close (the letter's "begin again" turn, the river's home-is-the-source turn), but both spend their length on itinerary; the winner spends its on one man, one boat, one morning.
